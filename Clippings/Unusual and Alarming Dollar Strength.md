This is a quick post to say something very unusual - and potentially alarming - is going on in markets. I’ve been banging the drum that Fed tightening in the face of falling oil prices is a [mistake](https://robinjbrooks.substack.com/p/the-fed-is-making-a-terrible-mistake) and that it risks driving the US into recession. Today’s market moves have the whiff of a credit event somewhere because we’re seeing very unusual Dollar strength that doesn’t easily map into obvious fundamentals, least of all expectations for Fed tightening, which have been relatively stable in recent days. I’m writing this post to alert my readers to this unusual price action. It is possible that something serious is going on. It’s also possible this is noise. So this about putting this unusual price action on your radar screen and isn’t something you should act on immediately because we don’t know what’s going on.

The chart above shows my standard two indices that track Dollar strength. The black line is the trade-weighted Dollar against the G10. The blue line is the trade-weighted Dollar against EM. It’s the latter that is my preferred measure for Dollar movement as the Dollar versus the G10 has tended to be more volatile this year and a less reliable leading indicator for Dollar direction going forward. As the chart shows, we’re today seeing a very sharp and unusual rise in the Dollar versus EM and the reasons for this are far from obvious, so there may be something going on behind the scenes.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2n7z!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc12d15f8-2ded-4ac0-9698-c879aa148557_491x431.png)

The reason I say it’s not clear what’s driving this Dollar strength should be clear from the chart above. It’s not easy to link the Dollar versus EM to rate differentials, but you can certainly do that for the Dollar versus the G10 and that’s what’s in this chart. The black line is the same as in the first chart. The blue line is the corresponding interest rate differential, where I am using 2y2y forward rates to proxy for where markets see terminal rates and I’m using the same trade-weights for the rate differential as for the G10 Dollar. It’s clear that the sharp rise in the Dollar has totally decoupled from the rate differential, so Dollar strength is NOT about the hawkish Fed. That can be a sign that something is else is going on, i.e. something is breaking.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vjl-!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe95fd907-10a7-4fff-8137-184f2bddea50_477x465.png)

It is not yet the case that there’s outright signs of Dollar shortage or panic in global markets. The blue line in the chart above is the cross-currency basis for EUR/$, while the black line is the same thing for $/JPY. When markets panic, as in the 2011/12 Euro zone debt crisis or during COVID, this cross-currency basis moves sharply negative as markets buy Dollars in the spot market and hedge that exposure back in the forward market. This is NOT happening at the current juncture, so we’ve not yet reached the all-out panic stage. We’re just seeing unusual Dollar strength in the spot market.

Of course, what’s happening with the Dollar isn’t going on in isolation. Gold fell over three percent today, while silver is down over five percent and copper fell a similar amount. I’ll write about what’s going on with precious metals for tomorrow’s post.
