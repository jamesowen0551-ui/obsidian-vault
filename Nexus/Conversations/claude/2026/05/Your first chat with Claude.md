> What is the difference between AI and ML?
<!-- UID: 019e6291-3675-7780-8d6d-21431404ef0c -->
>[!nexus_agent] **Assistant** - 2026/05/26 at 12:35:49
> AI (Artificial Intelligence) is the broader field focused on building systems that can perform tasks requiring human-like intelligence — reasoning, problem-solving, understanding language, recognizing patterns, and so on.
>
> ML (Machine Learning) is a *subset* of AI. Rather than being explicitly programmed with rules, ML systems learn patterns from data and improve through experience.
>
> A simple way to think about it: all ML is AI, but not all AI is ML.
>
> **Other subsets of AI** include things like expert systems (rule-based logic), robotics, and search algorithms — none of which necessarily involve machine learning.
>
> **Deep Learning** is then a subset of ML, using neural networks with many layers, and it powers most of today's headline AI breakthroughs (image recognition, LLMs like me, etc.).
>
> So the hierarchy looks like: **AI ⊃ ML ⊃ Deep Learning**.
